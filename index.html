Spreadsheet Scrambler

Anonymize an Excel file: make a copy with made-up names, numbers and dates but the same sheets, columns and formulas, so you can share it, test with it or hand it to other tools without exposing the real data.

Your file never leaves this computer. Everything happens inside this page, in your browser.

  • No upload and no server: the file is read and rewritten in memory.
  • No AI, LLM or agent, and no call to any provider or service.
  • No analytics, cookies or outside code. The page's security policy blocks every network request.
  • Works offline: save this page and open it from your disk.

Which cells

Leave empty for every cell. Or list ranges such as B2:F500, H:H, 3:10. Put a sheet name in front to limit a range to that sheet: Sales!A2:C40.

What is it for?
  • Sending a realistic sample to a vendor, consultant or support team without sending real customer or financial data.
  • Giving a spreadsheet to an AI assistant or another online tool so it can build formulas, reports or code, without it ever seeing the real values.
  • Test data for developers and data teams that has the exact shape of production files.
  • Demos, training and bug reports with files that look real but aren't.
How values change
  • Text: every letter becomes a random letter and every digit a random digit. Spaces, punctuation and length stay, and Arabic, Cyrillic, Chinese and other scripts stay in their own script. The same text always becomes the same made-up text, so lookups, filters and dropdown lists keep working.
  • Numbers: same sign and the same number of digits before and after the decimal point, with new digits. Zero stays zero.
  • Dates and times: moved by up to a year, earlier or later.
  • TRUE/FALSE: picked at random for each cell.
  • Comments on scrambled cells are scrambled too, and so are the copies of values that charts, pivot tables and links to other files keep inside the file.
What stays the same
  • Sheet names, layout, formatting, column widths, merged cells and table headings.
  • Formulas. Their saved results are cleared, because they could give the real values away. Excel works them out again when you open the file.
  • Text and numbers typed inside formulas, unless the text matches a value that was scrambled.
  • Pictures, embedded files and macros. The page tells you when the file has any.
Can it be undone?

No. The page takes its random choices from your browser's secure random number generator and never stores them, so nobody can work back to the original values, you included. Keep your original file.
